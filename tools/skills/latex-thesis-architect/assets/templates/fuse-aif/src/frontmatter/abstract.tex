\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}

Automated facial analysis systems are widely deployed despite well-documented accuracy disparities across demographic cohorts, yet independent and reproducible auditing remains rare in practice. This report introduces BiasAperture, a diagnostic software framework that audits third-party facial analysis models for subgroup and intersectional disparities and compiles them into regulator-ready compliance dossiers. The framework integrates five modular layers: demographic data ingestion, model interfacing, harmonized fairness metrics, surrogate explainability, and report generation. Its analytical core evaluates four canonical disparity metrics---Demographic Parity Difference, Equalized Odds Difference, Equal Opportunity Difference, and Disparate Impact Ratio---cross-validated between Fairlearn and AIF360 backends. Crucially, every reported disparity is evaluated with Pearson's $\chi^2$ significance tests and 95\% BCa bootstrap confidence intervals ($B \ge 1{,}000$) rather than uncalibrated point thresholds, with automated suppression guards enforced on sparse cohorts ($n < 30$). For interpretability, a surrogate attribution layer isolates whether flagged performance gaps stem from demographic attributes or proxy confounds, while pixel-level spatial SHAP remains explicitly deferred to post-capstone milestones. All findings compile into an air-gapped, standalone HTML audit report structured under Model Cards and Datasheets conventions, mapping every metric directly to Article~10 of the EU AI Act and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. We validate the platform on the FairFace benchmark ($N = 10{,}954$) using an audited ResNet-34 classifier baseline; UTKFace was profiled during exploratory analysis and descoped to maintain statistical consistency. BiasAperture is scoped strictly as diagnostic: it identifies and statistically characterises disparities without altering model weights, applying in-processing mitigations, or synthesizing demographic images.
