# Brainstorm Research Laboratory — Cohort 0 Overview

**Purpose:** Short introduction for invited Cohort 0 candidates.

## What is Brainstorm Research Laboratory?

Brainstorm Research Laboratory (BRL) is a small engineering and research guild built around **real technical work, small quests, reproducible artifacts, and verifiable contribution history**.

The goal is not to collect impressive titles. The goal is to learn by doing useful work and to leave behind artifacts that can be inspected, reproduced, and credited to the person who produced them.

## What is Cohort 0?

Cohort 0 (C0) is the initial small group used to operate and calibrate the BRL system.

C0 is intentionally small so that the workflow, teaching material, quest difficulty, review process, and communication model can be tested before the program is expanded.

## How the system works

```text
Join
  ↓
Understand the basics
  ↓
Receive a small Quest
  ↓
Investigate / build / collect
  ↓
Produce an artifact
  ↓
Review & verification
  ↓
Receive documented contribution credit
  ↓
Progress to harder Quests
```

A **Quest** is simply a bounded technical task with:

- a clear objective,
- defined output,
- evidence requirements, and
- a completion condition.

## Examples of C0 work

Depending on interests and capability, early work may include:

- collecting and structuring public information from Nepal,
- building or cleaning small datasets,
- testing and reproducing software behaviour,
- document and language-data investigation,
- benchmarking a technical component,
- preparing a small research experiment.

You are **not expected to already know everything required for a Quest**. C0 is designed so that people can learn the necessary tools and methods as they progress.

## What the progression means

```text
Unranked
   ↓
Rank E — Research Scout
   ↓
Rank D — Research Analyst
   ↓
Rank C — Research Engineer
   ↓
Rank B — Research Fellow
   ↓
Rank A — Senior Fellow
```

The ranks describe demonstrated capability. They are based on completed work and verification, not popularity or titles.

## What is expected?

BRL expects contributors to:

- be honest about what they actually observed or completed,
- keep sources and evidence traceable,
- learn unfamiliar tools when necessary,
- communicate blockers rather than hide them,
- accept review and improve their work.

AI tools may be used as productivity and learning aids, but submitted work must still be checked by the contributor.

## Time and cost

C0 is designed around a realistic part-time commitment. The current BRL plan uses approximately **5–8 hours per week** as the normal active-cohort range, with flexibility around academic workload.

There is **no mandatory paid software, subscription, hardware purchase, or participant contribution required to join C0**.

Participants are not expected to donate to BRL or finance their own assigned Quest in order to participate.

## Support and sponsorship

BRL may receive external support for shared laboratory needs such as infrastructure, compute, hardware, workshops, or research operations. It may also accept sponsorship directed toward a **specific research project**, including a project led by an individual contributor.

These are separate from participation:

```text
General BRL Support
→ strengthens shared laboratory infrastructure

Project Sponsorship
→ supports a defined project and its approved expenses

Individual-Led Project Sponsorship
→ supports a specific contributor-led project,
   not the contributor's general participation
```

Project sponsorship is tied to a defined project, budget, deliverables, and reporting process. Sponsorship does **not** by itself give a sponsor ownership of BRL, unrelated projects, contributor selection, or authority to determine research conclusions.

See [BRL Support & Sponsorship Framework](BRL_SUPPORT_AND_SPONSORSHIP.md) for the operating model.

## Equal opportunity

BRL aims to provide equal opportunity to participate in its research and engineering activities. Selection, Quest assignment, evaluation, progression, authorship, and other participation decisions are based on relevant capability, conduct, contribution, and program requirements.

## What happens next?

The first step is a short **Participant Intake & Placement Form**. It helps us understand your background, interests, available time, and current setup so that the first Quest can be matched appropriately.

Detailed technical onboarding, Git instructions, research methods, and the formal Contributor Agreement come **after** the initial intake.
