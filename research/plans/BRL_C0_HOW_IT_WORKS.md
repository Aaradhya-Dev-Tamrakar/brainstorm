# Brainstorm Research Laboratory — How Cohort 0 Works

**Purpose:** A one-page practical explanation for invited C0 candidates.

## Your path

### 1. Read the short introduction
Start with [BRL C0 Overview](BRL_C0_OVERVIEW.md).

You do not need to study the full research repository at this stage.

### 2. Complete the intake form

The form collects a small amount of information about:

- your current technical background,
- what you want to learn,
- the kinds of work that interest you,
- your laptop/software environment, and
- your realistic weekly availability.

This is used for **placement**, not as a theoretical exam.

### 3. Receive your first assignment

You will receive a small, clearly defined Quest.

A Quest normally tells you:

```text
Target
Objective
What to investigate/build
What evidence to keep
Expected output
How "done" is checked
```

For example:

> Investigate one assigned Nepal public institution and record a defined set of publicly observable properties.

You would not be expected to design the entire research system yourself.

### 4. Learn while doing the Quest

Before or during the Quest, you will receive the technical material needed for that stage.

This may include:

- basic Git/GitHub usage,
- data-collection methods,
- evidence and source handling,
- a small technical tutorial,
- examples of acceptable outputs.

The intended pattern is **small task → feedback → slightly harder task**, rather than a large lecture followed by a large project.

### 5. Submit your work

Your result may be a dataset, report, script, test result, benchmark, or another defined artifact.

The submission is reviewed for things such as:

- correctness,
- traceable evidence,
- completeness,
- reproducibility,
- technical discipline.

### 6. Progress

Completed work is recorded as part of your contributor history.

As demonstrated capability increases, Quests become more technically demanding and your Guild rank can advance.

## What review looks like

Review is part of the process.

A reviewer may ask you to:

- add a missing source,
- reproduce a result,
- correct a data field,
- explain how you obtained an observation,
- test an edge case.

A revision request does **not** mean the whole task failed. The purpose is to make the artifact reliable.

## Communication

Technical questions and blockers are intended to be visible to the cohort so that useful answers can benefit everyone.

When coursework, exams, or another major commitment temporarily prevents progress, BRL has a **[PAUSE]** mechanism so that work can be stopped and resumed cleanly.

## What you do not need before joining

You do not need:

- prior research publications,
- advanced machine learning knowledge,
- advanced Git expertise,
- experience with the whole Brainstorm ecosystem,
- a perfect project portfolio.

You do need a willingness to learn, investigate carefully, and document what you actually did.

## After the intake

Candidates who proceed to C0 receive the formal onboarding package, including the Contributor Agreement, Quest 0, technical setup instructions, and the first assigned Quest.
