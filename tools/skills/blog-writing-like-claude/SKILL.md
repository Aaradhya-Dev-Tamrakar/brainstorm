# Blog Writing Like Claude — The "Calm Authority" Engineering Standard

This skill codifies the writing philosophy, voice, structural mechanics, and editorial standards of the official **Claude / Anthropic engineering blog** (`claude.com/blog`). It transforms technical writing—across blog posts, case studies, product announcements, tutorials, portfolio entries, and project documentation—into authoritative, high-trust, hype-free communication.

---

## 1. Core Philosophy: The Whiteboard Test

The central premise of Claude-style writing is **calm authority**:
> **Write like you are explaining a real system to a smart colleague at a whiteboard. Not selling. Not performing. Just explaining.**

Trust is not generated by superlative adjectives or enthusiastic exclamation marks. Trust is generated by:
$$\text{Trust} = \text{Precision} + \text{Scope Boundaries} + \text{Empirical Proof}$$

### The Three Pillars
1. **Dog-Fooding Transparency**: Tell your own story using your own tool. Document friction, limitations, and iterations honestly.
2. **Functional Precision**: Define concepts by what they *are* and *do* in concrete mechanical terms, never with abstract marketing jargon.
3. **Scope Honesty**: Proactively declare what the system *cannot* do. Stating non-goals and explicit boundaries is the ultimate signal of technical credibility.

## 2. The 10 Invariant Writing Rules

Every document drafted or audited under this skill must comply with these ten invariants:

### Rule 1: Title = Statement of Factual State
- Titles are complete sentences or precise noun phrases that declare what happened or what exists.
- **Never** use clickbait questions, numbered listicles, or emotional teases.
- ❌ *"Is AI ready to transform government agencies? The answer may surprise you."*
- ❌ *"10 Game-Changing Features in Our New Release"*
- ✅ *"Claude for Government is now generally available"*
- ✅ *"How Anthropic's sales team rebuilt inbound with Claude Managed Agents"*
- ✅ *"Customize Claude Code with mods"*

### Rule 2: The Promise in the First Sentence (1-Sentence Thesis)
- The meta description or opening sentence must be a complete, self-contained thesis.
- If the reader closes the tab after the first paragraph, they must still understand the core insight and factual state change.
- ❌ *"In today's fast-paced AI landscape, organizations face unprecedented challenges when deploying models at scale. In this blog post, we will explore..."*
- ✅ *"How a Claude-powered buying agent now answers most inbound customers, and how that changed the way our sales team works."*

### Rule 3: Strict Hype Blacklist & Active Verbs
- Ban all inflated marketing superlatives. Replace adjectives with measurable nouns and active verbs.
- Banned vocabulary: *revolutionary, game-changing, groundbreaking, unleash, supercharge, seamless, paradigm shift, next-gen, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art (unless citing benchmark leaderboards with SOTA metrics), magic, effortlessly*.
- ❌ *"Unleash the full power of our revolutionary, game-changing fairness engine."*
- ✅ *"BiasAperture audits facial analysis models for demographic bias across four parity metrics and generates standalone compliance dossiers."*

### Rule 4: Functional Precision in Definition
- Define any feature, tool, or concept in 10 to 15 concrete, mechanical words.
- ❌ *"Mods provide an ultra-flexible, powerful extensibility framework for developer customization."*
- ✅ *"Mods are small TypeScript functions that change how Claude Code works."*

### Rule 5: Explicit Scope Boundaries & Non-Goals
- Dedicate explicit prose to what the feature or project **does not** do.
- Explicitly differentiate release states: **General Availability (GA)** vs. **Early Access / Preview** vs. **Deferred Roadmap**.
- Acknowledge constraints naturally in the text, not buried in small-print footnotes.
- ✅ *"BiasAperture is strictly diagnostic: it measures disparity and calculates statistical significance. It does not retrain models, alter weights, or generate synthetic training data."*

### Rule 6: Dog-Fooding as Primary Proof
- Use your own product on your own codebase, data, or workflows, and publish the verified outputs.
- Acknowledge nuances with honest modifiers (*"most inbound customers"*, not *"all customers"*).
- Presenting real benchmark distributions and admitting where a baseline struggles builds unshakeable credibility.

### Rule 7: Sentence & Paragraph Geometry
- **One idea per paragraph.** Paragraphs should rarely exceed 3 to 4 sentences.
- **One action per sentence.** Avoid long chains of dependent clauses connected by semicolons or trailing participles.
- Favor active voice with simple subjects and strong verbs.

### Rule 8: Functional, Copy-Pasteable Code
- Code blocks must be syntactically valid, self-contained, and immediately runnable.
- Include explicit imports, realistic variable names, and expected outputs.
- Never use hand-waving placeholder comments like `// implement your logic here` without providing the surrounding functional context.

### Rule 9: Machine-Readable Metadata (Structured Data)
- Every web article or report must pair human-readable prose with machine-readable schema markup (`schema.org` JSON-LD with `@type: "BlogPosting"`, `"TechArticle"`, or `"Report"`).
- Include canonical URLs, deterministic timestamps, and clean social metadata (OG images at 1200×630).

### Rule 10: Actionable Next Steps over Redundant Conclusions
- Do not conclude with a paragraph that simply restates what was already written.
- End with concrete next steps: installation commands, links to documentation, code repositories, or open questions.

## 3. The Three Content Archetypes

When authoring a piece, identify which archetype fits the goal and follow its structural formula:

### Archetype A: The Internal Case Study / Dog-Fooding
*Used for:* Engineering deep dives, internal system overhauls, or architecture post-mortems.
- **Voice:** Reflective, first-person plural (*"our team"*), candid about mistakes and iteration.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Title:** *"How [Team] [Solved/Rebuilt X] with [System/Tool]"*
  2. **1-Sentence Thesis Subtitle:** What changed and why the reader should care.
  3. **The Before State:** The baseline process, operational friction, and specific numbers (e.g. queue latency, error rate).
  4. **The Architecture:** What was built, concrete components, and diagrammed system interactions.
  5. **Empirical Results:** Measured outcomes with specific figures (*"answers 68% of inbound inquiries with zero human handoff"*).
  6. **Warts & Edge Cases:** What failed during early iterations and how the team adapted.
  7. **How It Changed How We Work:** Cultural or workflow shifts resulting from the change.

### Archetype B: The Product / Feature Announcement
*Used for:* Release notes, GA announcements, new tool launches, or framework milestones.
- **Voice:** Factual, understated confidence, scope-precise.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Title:** *"[Product/Feature] is now [generally available / in early access]"*
  2. **1-Sentence Thesis Subtitle:** Summarize the release and secondary highlights.
  3. **Core Capabilities:** 3 to 4 bullet points, each starting with an active imperative verb (*"Rewrite", "Block", "Add", "Replace"*).
  4. **Scope & Availability Matrix:** Clean table or callout distinguishing GA vs. Early Access features.
  5. **Concrete Code / Configuration Example:** Minimal working snippet showing the feature in action.
  6. **Access & Getting Started:** Exact CLI commands or signup URLs.

### Archetype C: The Developer Tutorial / Guide
*Used for:* How-to guides, API references, customization manuals, and walkthroughs.
- **Voice:** Direct, instructional, second-person (*"you"*), task-focused.
- **Structure:**
  1. **Title:** *"How to [Accomplish Specific Task] [with Tool]"* or *"Customize [Tool] with [Feature]"*
  2. **1-Sentence Definition:** Define what the entity is in 11 words or fewer.
  3. **Prerequisites & Setup:** Exact runtime requirements.
  4. **Step-by-Step Implementation:** Code snippets accompanied by brief 1-2 sentence rationales.
  5. **Common Pitfalls & Guardrails:** Edge cases, validation errors, and debugging commands.
  6. **Next Steps:** References to deeper APIs or companion repositories.

## 4. Multi-Surface Application Matrix

This skill applies across diverse technical writing surfaces:

| Writing Surface | Common Failure Mode | Claude-Style Transformation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **GitHub README** | 60-word opening sentence packed with buzzwords and grand visions. | **20-word thesis**: *"BiasAperture audits facial analysis models for demographic bias and generates regulator-ready compliance reports."* Followed by a 4-bullet capabilities list. |
| **Academic Abstract** | Run-on clauses with 50+ words per sentence, defensive hedging. | **Split sentences**: Make claims standalone. Celebrate explicit scope cuts (*"UTKFace was profiled and cut from implementation scope"*). |
| **Portfolio Case Study** | Passive resume-style bullet points (*"Assisted in developing..."*). | **Dog-fooding narrative**: *"We audited our own baseline model. Here is what we found, the exact 95% confidence intervals, and what we cut."* |
| **Generated Compliance Reports** | Bare numbers with zero context or defensive boilerplate. | **Calm executive cards**: 4-stat metric strip, clear `$n < 30$` suppression callouts, and explicit *"Scope & Non-Intervention"* guarantees. |
| **Issue / PR Descriptions** | Ambiguous summaries (*"Update tests and fix bugs"*). | **Factual state changes**: *"fix(fairness): suppress metric values for cohorts with sample size n < 30"*. |

## 6. Pre-Publishing Audit Checklist

Before releasing any piece of writing, run through this verification checklist:

- [ ] **Title Check**: Is the title a factual statement of state change rather than a clickbait question or tease?
- [ ] **Thesis Check**: Does the first sentence state the complete takeaway?
- [ ] **Hype Scan**: Are words like *revolutionary, game-changing, unleash, seamless, cutting-edge* completely eliminated?
- [ ] **Scope Boundary Check**: Is there an explicit statement of what this does **not** do?
- [ ] **Number Verification**: Is every empirical claim backed by an exact number, percentage, or confidence interval?
- [ ] **Paragraph Geometry**: Is each paragraph restricted to 1–4 sentences with a single core focus?
- [ ] **Code Verifiability**: Can every provided code snippet be copied and executed without unseen dependencies?
- [ ] **Actionable Conclusion**: Does the ending provide concrete next steps instead of repeating the introduction?
