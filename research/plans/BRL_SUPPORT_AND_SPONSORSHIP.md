# Brainstorm Research Laboratory — Support & Sponsorship Framework

**Status:** Program framework  
**Applies to:** BRL shared operations, research programs, and contributor-led projects

## 1. Purpose

BRL can be supported by individuals, companies, institutions, or other partners through resources that help research and engineering work happen.

The framework keeps three things separate:

1. **general support for BRL,**
2. **sponsorship of a defined research program or project,**
3. **sponsorship of a specific contributor-led project.**

Support is a resource mechanism, not a mechanism for purchasing research outcomes, contributor standing, or control over BRL.

## 2. General BRL Support

General support strengthens the shared laboratory rather than a single project.

Examples include:

- development hardware and components,
- compute or API credits,
- software or infrastructure services,
- fabrication and workshop resources,
- shared research operations,
- cohort or educational activities.

General support may be provided as money, equipment, services, credits, or other in-kind resources, subject to the applicable administrative and legal arrangements.

A general supporter does not automatically acquire rights over individual projects, contributor work, research results, or BRL governance.

## 3. Research or Program Sponsorship

A sponsor may support a defined BRL research program or a bounded program of work.

A sponsorship record should identify, as applicable:

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Program / Project ID | Exact supported initiative |
| Sponsor | Individual, company, institution, or other entity |
| Support | Cash and/or in-kind support |
| Intended use | Approved categories of expenditure |
| Project lead | Responsible technical lead |
| Deliverables | Expected artifacts or milestones |
| Reporting | How use of resources and progress are documented |
| Attribution | How sponsorship is acknowledged |
| IP / output terms | Applicable ownership, licensing, and publication terms |
| End / unspent handling | What happens when the project ends |

## 4. Individual-Led Project Sponsorship

A contributor may propose or lead a project that is separately sponsored.

The model is:

```text
Sponsor
   ↓
Defined Project
   ↓
Project Budget / Resources
   ↓
Project Lead + Contributors
   ↓
Technical Deliverables
   ↓
Report / Artifact / Demonstration
```

The sponsorship is attached to the **project**, not to the contributor's general membership.

A project lead may therefore receive project resources for legitimate project expenses such as components, fabrication, compute, testing, travel directly required by the project, or other approved costs.

Unless a separate lawful and documented arrangement says otherwise, project sponsorship should not be represented as a general salary, stipend, prize, or personal payment.

## 5. Sponsor Independence

Sponsors provide resources; they do not automatically control the research.

Sponsorship does not by itself grant a sponsor:

- ownership of BRL,
- ownership of unrelated projects,
- authority over contributor selection or promotion,
- authority to assign or remove contributors,
- ownership of unrelated contributor-created work,
- the right to require a predetermined research result,
- authority to suppress inconvenient findings.

A project may define legitimate sponsor requirements such as a reporting schedule, a permitted budget, a delivery format, or a specific practical objective. These requirements must be documented before resources are committed.

## 6. Contributor Participation

Contributors are **not required to donate, pay membership fees, purchase equipment, or secure sponsorship** in order to participate in C0.

A lack of personal financial resources must not itself become a barrier to completing a standard Quest. Where a project genuinely needs resources beyond the contributor's available means, the project should use shared BRL resources, free-tier infrastructure, in-kind support, or an appropriate sponsorship mechanism where available.

## 7. Transparency and Attribution

Where support or sponsorship is used, BRL should maintain a traceable record of:

- who provided the support,
- what was supported,
- what resources were provided,
- what project or program received them,
- material deliverables produced, and
- relevant attribution.

Public acknowledgement of a sponsor should be agreed with the sponsor and should not misrepresent the scope of their support.

Contributors retain attribution for work they actually author. Sponsorship does not replace or erase technical authorship.

## 8. Financial and Legal Handling

BRL should only receive, hold, spend, or distribute funds through an appropriate legal and administrative arrangement.

The existence of this framework does **not** imply that every form of support is tax-deductible, charitable, or subject to the same accounting treatment. Those questions depend on the legal status of the receiving entity, the source and form of support, and applicable rules.

Where a formal sponsorship involves material funds, equipment, institutional partners, or external funding sources, the terms should be documented before acceptance.

## 9. Separation from Merit

Financial support must remain separate from contributor evaluation.

A contributor does not receive higher technical standing, Quest priority, authorship, promotion, or other merit recognition merely because they or someone connected to them provided funding.

Likewise, a sponsor does not receive research credit that has not been earned through actual contribution.
